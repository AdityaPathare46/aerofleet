\opening{Dear Editor,}

We submit the manuscript ``Evidence, not verdicts: what 590 public PX4 crash logs can and cannot tell a drone fleet operator'' as a research article.

Flight logs are increasingly used to decide why a small drone crashed, who was responsible, and which aircraft to
inspect next. We tested those uses on the largest public archive of real autopilot logs (PX4 Flight Review, 479,547
logs). Each confirmatory test was committed to version control with its pass mark before it was run;
analyses added during review are marked as exploratory.

Two findings are, to our knowledge, new and apply to any evaluation of log-based diagnostics:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Evaluation choices shape the apparent signal.} In a factorial audit of 32 evaluation settings, a
crash-vs-normal AUC of 0.88 fell by 0.21 [0.06, 0.34]; the one choice that could be isolated, grouping
cross-validation folds by vehicle, accounted for 0.06. Comparing each drone with its own earlier flights brought it to chance.
\item \textbf{Archive records need care.} The usual tilt rule finds no crash-like tilt in 61\% of crash-rated
multicopter logs and misses roughly 7--50\% of crashes, depending on the rater; pilot-rated logs show one less often (23\% vs
45\%); and dates cannot order flights within a day.
\end{itemize}
These rest on declared tests:
\begin{itemize}
\item neither a declared rule nor cross-validated models separated pilot-caused from aircraft-caused crashes
(balanced accuracy 0.47 on 112 held-out crashes), nor recovered a blind relabelling;
\item crash-window leakage was small overall but material among multicopter crashes with a crash-like tilt;
\item the flight that crashed was no different from the same drone's earlier flights, whereas drones that later
crashed vibrated more than matched drones on ordinary flights, a suggestive signal ($p=0.012$ per airframe,
0.076 per operator site) that did not clearly beat random inspection against 412 non-crash drones;
\item a re-implementation of a published trained fault detector fell from AUC 0.94 to 0.63 once whole
days were held out.
\end{itemize}
The findings feed a reporting checklist and an open-source tool that presents evidence rather than verdicts;
code, declarations, derived features and labels are released.

The manuscript is original, has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere. All authors approve the
submission. An AI assistant was used in preparing code and text, as declared in the manuscript.
