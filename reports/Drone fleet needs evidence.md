# Rules, not crash data, justify AeroFleet

There is a real, evidence-backed need, but it is narrower than the current AeroFleet 2.0 feature list and it comes from regulation, not from accident statistics. The strongest proven need is **tamper-evident record-keeping for operators flying under an authorisation**: UK/EU specific-category operators must keep a per-aircraft electronic technical log for **3 years**, including "a completed pre-flight inspection" and "any repairs and changes to the UAS configuration", stored so it is protected from "alteration" ([CAA UK Reg 2019/947](https://regulatorylibrary.caa.co.uk/2019-947-pdf/PDF.pdf)). Existing logbook tools are built mainly around DJI log sync, so ArduPilot/PX4 fleets are poorly served. Accident data does **not** back a "wrong settings cause crashes" pitch: no official investigator or academic dataset found gives any share for operator misconfiguration, and the only insurer data puts pilot error first at **33% of claims** ([DroneXL](https://dronexl.co/2022/02/02/drone-pilot-error-insurance-claim/)). India is weak ground for now: no public DGCA incident data, no confirmed link between Indian fleets and ArduPilot/PX4, and field complaints are about batteries and repair delays, not settings. The best pivot is to make AeroFleet an **ArduPilot/PX4 evidence pack**: it builds the technical-log entries a regulator can audit (firmware, parameter changes, pre-flight check, flight inside the approved area) straight from .bin/.ulg files, in a hash-chained record. Settings drift becomes one field in that record, not the headline. Planned-vs-flown route comparison, mission conflict checks and the wind/battery check have no support in the evidence gathered.

## Accident data cannot carry a "settings drift" pitch

Investigators record *what happened*, not *which setting was wrong*. The UK AAIB names loss of control in flight as the main drone accident type, "usually resulting from the UAS becoming unresponsive" or behaving erratically. Its categories (loss of control, component failure, power failure, data-link loss, collision) have no slot for a wrong parameter ([AAIB ASR 2023](https://www.suasnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/AAIB-Annual-Safety-Review-2023.pdf)). Academic studies disagree on the basic split. Australian occurrence data leans technical, with failed equipment the largest group ([ECU, Wild et al.](https://ro.ecu.edu.au/ecuworkspost2013/2410)). A small HFACS coding of NASA ASRS reports leans human, at **78% human vs 20% hardware/software** ([ScholarWorks](https://scholarworks.uark.edu/hnrcsturpc25/29)); that is a student poster, so give it little weight. No dataset codes configuration error as its own cause. The missing number does not prove the problem is rare. It does mean the team cannot quote a rate.

Individual cases show configuration *can* matter, and these are the ones to cite. In the Orlando drone show (Dec 2024), the NTSB preliminary report found that the launch parameter file with the final flight paths had not been sent to the drones before multiple drones fell into the crowd ([WKMG](https://gmg-wkmg-prod.cdn.arcpublishing.com/news/local/2025/01/16/crashed-drones-in-orlando-christmas-show-were-not-getting-data-minutes-before-show-ntsb-report-finds)). A Swiss STSB recommendation concerns an aircraft running **ArduCopter 3.5.0-rc5**, a release candidate that could use only 2 of its 3 IMUs ([STSB 588](https://www.sust.admin.ch/en/safety-recommendations/aviation/av_se_588.pdf)); this is known only from a search snippet and is unverified. Software studies confirm that parameter handling is a real bug class in PX4 and ArduPilot, with **1,000+ parameters** in PX4 ([FSE'21](https://yuleisui.github.io/publications/fse21.pdf)), and ArduPilot's own documentation shows one setting (`FS_EKF_THRESH=0`) that switches off a failsafe ([ArduPilot docs](https://ardupilot.org/copter/docs/common-ekf-inav-failsafe.html)). But those are developer bugs and theoretical risks, not counted field incidents.

The causes investigators keep repeating are pre-flight condition and automation behaviour: batteries not latched or with heat-damaged connectors, return-to-home not climbing high enough, a home point not set properly ([AAIB ASR 2023](https://www.suasnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/AAIB-Annual-Safety-Review-2023.pdf); [AAIB Inspire 2](https://www.gov.uk/aaib-reports/aaib-investigation-to-dji-inspire-2-uas-registration-n-a-080220)). Most of these are physical faults that software can only record, not detect. I found **no incident at all** where the cause was a drone departing from its planned route.

## Regulation creates the clearest, auditable obligation

The UK/EU specific category is where the demand is written down. UAS.SPEC.050(1)(g) requires 3-year records of maintenance and of operations "including any unusual technical or operational occurrences". The UK AMC adds the per-aircraft electronic technical log with defined fields (configuration changes, pre-flight inspection, defects, hours and cycles) and protection from alteration. The maintenance log "may be requested for inspection/audit by the CAA during oversight activities". Pre-flight rules require the pilot to confirm that the software is "the latest version published by the UAS manufacturer" ([CAA UK Reg 2019/947](https://regulatorylibrary.caa.co.uk/2019-947-pdf/PDF.pdf)). Taken together, this is a legal duty to show, per flight, which configuration flew and that it was approved, kept in a form that cannot be quietly changed. AeroFleet's hash-chained record and settings comparison map onto that duty directly. One caveat: these fields are the UK AMC wording, and the EASA version was not checked.

In the US, Part 107 only requires an accident report within **10 days** and records on request ([eCFR Part 107](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/part-107)). The proposed Part 108 BVLOS rule would require maintenance and mechanical-irregularity records for 24 months or the life of the aircraft ([Federal Register NPRM](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/08/07/2025-14992/normalizing-unmanned-aircraft-systems-beyond-visual-line-of-sight-operations)), but it was still at OIRA with no final rule as of Aug 2026 ([UAS Dashboard](https://uasdashboard.com/guides/part-108-guide)). Aloft has already shipped maintenance tooling aimed at Part 108 operators ([Unmanned Airspace](https://www.unmannedairspace.info/uncategorized/aloft-releases-maintenance-scheduling-tool/)), so the space will be contested.

India's Drone Rules 2021 ask for much less. The firm duties are a mandatory Digital Sky check before each flight (Rule 21), permission for red/yellow zones (Rule 22, whose breach is non-compoundable), conformance to a type certificate (Rule 14(3)) and an accident report within **48 hours** (Rule 30) ([Drone Rules 2021](https://www.legitquest.com/act/drone-rules-2021/8A40)). NPNT, tracking and geofencing are only *enabling* powers under Rule 12, and I found no rule requiring flight logs to be uploaded. Agri sprayers reportedly must submit spray-monitoring data within 7 days ([CropLife India SOP](https://croplifeindia.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/Drone-SOP-India.pdf)), but the primary text was not read. No enforcement data linked to record-keeping clauses was found in any country, so the team cannot yet show that auditors actually fail operators on these points.

## Operators complain about batteries and paperwork, not parameters

Indian field reports describe practical problems. Drone Didi operators get 8–9 minutes of spraying per battery, wait about 2 hours to recharge, pay **₹40–50k** for a replacement battery, and waited **10–15 days** for a technician after a GPS fault ([Ground Report](https://www.groundreport.in/states-madhya-pradesh/namo-drone-didi-struggling-with-battery-and-servicing-issues-ground-report/)). The fleet is small and spread out. There are about **29,501** registered drones (Jan 2025) ([RS PQ](https://rsdebate.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/758334/1/PQ_267_10022025_U646_p134_p136.pdf)), and only **89 of 1,066** allocated Drone Didi drones had been distributed in Madhya Pradesh ([Ground Report](https://www.groundreport.in/states-madhya-pradesh/namo-drone-didi-struggling-with-battery-and-servicing-issues-ground-report/)). Large fleets belong to a few firms such as Marut, which claims 750+ drones ([Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/companies/marut_drones)). No public source ties any major Indian operator to ArduPilot or PX4. An India-first ArduPilot pitch therefore rests on an unverified assumption.

Globally, the hard numbers are old. In 2019, about **40%** of US public-safety drone programmes checked for firmware updates monthly at best, which the survey called a "potential safety risk" ([DRONERESPONDERS](https://www.droneresponders.org/_files/ugd/ef6978_19517dfe38b34fad94d29c2b0c908491.pdf)). The NTSB's own 12-drone programme kept records in spreadsheets it called "archaic" ([AvWeb](https://avweb.com/aviation-news/ntsb-works-to-streamline-drone-program/)). Industry surveys rank regulation, clients and funding as the top challenges, not fleet operations ([Heliguy](https://www.heliguy.com/blogs/posts/global-state-of-drones-2025-industry-whitepaper/)). For ArduPilot users, the visible pain is reading crash logs: help threads recur for years ([ArduPilot Discourse](https://discuss.ardupilot.org/t/crash-log-analysis-help/4643)), and a 2026 ML log diagnoser was publicly corrected by experienced forum members for wrong root causes ([ArduPilot Discourse 142646](https://discuss.ardupilot.org/t/introducing-an-ai-assisted-log-diagnosis-engine-tracing-root-causes-in-bin-files/142646)). That favours AeroFleet's deterministic, no-AI approach, but forum users are mostly not paying fleet customers.

## Ranked candidate needs

Existing tools cover logbooks, checklists, maintenance schedules and battery cycles, mostly built around DJI. DJI's end of US log sync in June 2024 exposed that dependence ([Aloft](https://www.aloft.ai/blog/flight-logging-import-with-aloft-whats-next-after-dji-sync/)). Airdata accepts ArduPilot logs, and FlyFreely checks logs against area and height limits; neither publicly describes fleet-wide parameter baselines. Funding is thin, and Verizon shut Skyward down in 2022 ([DroneLife](https://dronelife.com/2022/05/05/verizon-skyward-closed-drone-software-business-yesterday/)), a warning that this market is small.

### What the evidence does not support in AeroFleet 2.0

**Planned-vs-flown route comparison** has no incident evidence. Regulators ask whether the flight stayed inside the approved *volume* (UAS.SPEC.050(1)(f), India Rule 22), not whether it followed the route leg by leg, and FlyFreely already checks area and limits. Shrink this to a "stayed inside the approved area" check. **Mission conflict checks** and the **wind-at-altitude battery check** appear nowhere in the accident, regulatory or pain-point evidence; weather ranks 9th of 10 in the one insurer's claims list ([DroneDJ](https://dronedj.com/2022/02/04/coverdrone-drone-insurance-claims/)). Drop both or leave them as extras. **Settings drift as the headline** is supported only by rules and two or three cases, not by any frequency figure. Recast it as the "configuration changes" field of the technical log. **In-flight setting changes** fit the same AMC field and are worth keeping as a sub-feature. **The hash-chained signed record** is the best-supported idea because the UK AMC explicitly requires protection from alteration, but it is the foundation of the product, not the product.

### Unverified items to flag

All of the following are unverified: the autopilot stack of Indian fleets; the EASA (as opposed to UK) AMC field list; the full STSB 588 and the final NTSB Orlando report; NPNT enforcement status; the CIB&RC primary text; the Part 108 final text; any enforcement or audit-finding data; whether Airdata or FlyFreely already track ArduPilot parameter changes; and the count of ArduPilot log-help threads. The 64% "technical" share and the 78% "human" share both come from secondary or low-weight sources.

## Conclusion

The case for AeroFleet changes from "settings drift causes crashes", which no dataset can show, to "operators must prove what configuration flew, and nobody builds that proof for ArduPilot/PX4", which the UK/EU rules state directly. That makes the UK/EU specific category, not India, the first market to test. India becomes a later option once someone confirms which autopilots Indian fleets run and whether DGCA ever enforces log or configuration records.

The decisive test is cheap and not yet done. Show two or three UK/EU specific-category operators running ArduPilot/PX4 an auto-filled technical-log entry, built from one of their own logs, and ask whether it replaces work they do today and what they would pay. At the same time, ask Airdata and FlyFreely whether they already record ArduPilot parameter changes. If the operators shrug, or Airdata already does this, the remaining space is too small to be worth building.
